The problem

A yearly trend hides the season that matters

2×

Winter NO₂ over Tashkent is about twice the summer level, and winter is when NASA’s satellite sees the city least. Is it rising? The answer depends on the season, and on how much was seen.

Winter

A usable satellite reading on only 35% of days.

Summer

A usable satellite reading on 71% of days.

The gap

Most trend tools give one slope and one p-value. They do not show how much of each season was seen.

NO₂ = nitrogen dioxide, a gas from burning fuel in traffic, heating and power plants. Usable reading = a day when the satellite measured NO₂ over the city through less than 30% cloud. p-value = how easily chance alone could give this result.

Our analysis of NASA OMI data, 2004–2026. Context: Tashkent’s yearly PM2.5, a different pollutant, is about six times the WHO guideline (World Bank, 2024).
